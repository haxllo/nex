## The panel is now made of glass

Nex can now open as a real pane of glass instead of a painted panel. Turn it on in **Settings → Display → Background** and pick **Liquid Glass**. Everything else keeps working the way it did.

## What changed

- **Liquid Glass background**: the panel is a live, blurred and tinted window onto whatever is behind it — your wallpaper, a document, a browser — instead of a flat dark or white rectangle. Edges catch a thin rim of light and the surface picks up the colours underneath it, so the panel looks like it belongs to your desktop rather than sitting on top of it.
- **The glass is on your terms**: it stays off until you choose it. Black and white panel backgrounds are unchanged, and switching back is instant in the same settings page.
- **Opening no longer flickers**: before, the window could appear fully see-through for up to a second while it worked out what to show. It now stays solid until the glass has something real to show, then reveals.
- **A ripple of light as it opens**: the panel's edge glass settles into shape over about a fifth of a second instead of snapping on. If you dislike it, it is a one-line change in the code, not a setting, so say the word.
- **The panel keeps up with a changing desktop**: move a bright window behind the panel and the glass lightens with it. Text stays readable either way, and the change is a smooth fade, not a jump.
- **Glass keeps its last picture across opening**: closing and reopening used to sometimes flash the desktop from a moment ago. The panel now holds a consistent frame from the moment you last looked at it.
- **One press is one press**: for the first ten seconds after starting Nex, a single hotkey tap could open and close the panel, or need a second tap to react. A tap is now a single toggle from the very first second, every time, including over admin windows like Task Manager.
- **No more phantom hover**: when you opened the panel with the pointer somewhere else, it used to highlight whichever row the mouse had been over last time, which then looked like the highlight jumped. The panel now starts neutral unless the pointer is actually over it.

### Search and settings

- **More system pages**: 129 more Settings pages, plus Control Panel and MMC pages. Searches like "device", "storage" or "mouse" now reach the real page with its own icon and description instead of a generic gear or a missing icon.
- **Icons match the page**: the pages come with their official icons — Storage shows a disk, Sound shows a speaker, and so on, instead of a set of stand-ins.
- **One entry per page**: the same page can be reached by several names; Nex now shows it once, under its proper name.

### Notes

- Glass needs a graphics stack that all supported Windows versions have, and works on any monitor. On a monitor with HDR turned on, the glass is built from a normal-range copy of the screen; it still looks right, it is just not HDR.
- Content that Windows blocks from being copied (some protected video) comes through as a flat area behind the glass. That is Windows protecting it, not Nex failing to draw.
- If the panel is pushed right against a monitor edge, the glass can occasionally hold its previous frame for a moment. Everything else is unaffected.
